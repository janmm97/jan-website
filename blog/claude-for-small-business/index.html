What Claude for Small Business actually does, and what you still have to set up

October 26, 20265 min read1,019 words

The short answer: Claude for Small Business is a plugin you switch on inside Claude Cowork. Anthropic announced it on 13 May 2026 with connectors for tools like QuickBooks, PayPal and HubSpot, 15 ready-to-run workflows and 15 skills, and you approve before anything sends, posts or pays. Which jobs it takes on, and who signs off, is still yours to set.

What is Claude for Small Business?

Anthropic describes it as "a package of connectors and ready-to-run workflows that put Claude inside the tools small businesses depend on" (Anthropic, 13 May 2026). Setup starts simply. In Anthropic's words, "Claude for Small Business is a toggle install": you turn it on in Claude Cowork and connect your tools.

The announcement names seven tools: Intuit QuickBooks, PayPal, HubSpot, Canva, Docusign, Google Workspace and Microsoft 365.

What can it do out of the box?

At launch Anthropic listed 15 ready-to-run agentic workflows across finance, operations, sales, marketing, HR and customer service, plus 15 skills built on repetitive tasks. The examples on the page are practical ones: planning payroll, closing the month, a business pulse dashboard and running a campaign, along with an invoice chaser, a margin analyzer, a month-end prepper, a tax-season organizer, a contract reviewer, a lead triager and a content strategist (Anthropic).

It has grown since May. On 15 September 2026, Forbes reported that Anthropic added 43 workflows and 27 integrations, taking it to 37 partner connectors, including Shopify, Salesforce, TikTok Ads, Xero, Gusto, Square and Stripe. The same article says the plugin "has been installed more than 900,000 times since its May 13 launch, according to the company" (Forbes, 15 Sep 2026). Note that the install figure comes from Anthropic; Forbes doesn't say it checked it independently.

Lina Ochman, Anthropic's head of small business for the U.S., told Forbes she calls it "an agentic starter pack". Forbes also describes an onboarding step where Claude interviews the owner about the business.

What does it cost?

Not reported. Anthropic's announcement doesn't give a price for Claude for Small Business. Forbes reports that it "works on every paid Claude plan", so you'll need one of those, but I haven't seen a separate price for the plugin itself.

Does it act on its own?

Not by default. Anthropic's page says "you approve before anything sends, posts, or pays," and adds that you can approve a plan first or let a workflow run end to end. Anthropic's own tutorial puts it more plainly: "skills draft, propose, and stage," and for reminders, "Nothing goes out until you say so" (Claude Academy). Forbes describes the same thing as a default setting that stages every email, post or payment for approval before it goes out.

I like that default, because it's the rule my own agents run on. Nothing they make is posted, sent, published, bought or deleted until I approve it.

Is Claude better for starting a business?

It depends on where your work already lives, and I haven't run a fair side-by-side test of AI assistants, so I won't rank them. What I can say is narrower. If your books sit in QuickBooks, your payments in PayPal and your contacts in HubSpot, Claude for Small Business plugs straight into tools you already pay for, and that matters more on day one than any benchmark.

The question I'd ask first is a different one: do you know which jobs you want off your plate? If you can name them, a plugin like this gives you a head start. If you can't, no assistant will pick them for you.

What do you still have to set up?

The plugin hands you connectors and workflows. Four decisions stay with you, and I'll show how I handle each one with my own team of Claude Code agents, the JWS agents.

1. Which jobs to hand over. Each of my agents has exactly one job, written into its file. Hazel researches small businesses that could use automation and drafts outreach. Ivy finds the week's AI model news with dated sources. Felix makes the videos. Claude for Small Business gives you 15 launch workflows to choose from, and you still decide which of your jobs they take.

2. Which accounts it touches. My agents each get a list of apps and an access level in each one, read only or read and write. Hazel's Gmail access is drafts only and her Google Calendar access is read only, while Finn can read GitHub but not change it. Anthropic's tutorial says "Your existing permissions hold" and that Claude "can't see data you don't already have access to." That cuts both ways: whatever the account you connect can see, Claude can see too, so pick that account on purpose.

3. Who approves what. The plugin's approval default covers the moment something goes out. You still have to decide who in your business gives that approval. Payments, customer emails and social posts might belong to different people, and the sources I read don't say how the plugin handles more than one approver. For me it's simple, because I approve everything.

4. The rules for everything outside the connectors. My agents carry hard limits in their files, things they must never do whatever the task says. Hazel never sends, books or quotes prices, and Finn never writes a price into an offer. For your own setup, write down what Claude shouldn't touch, and decide what happens with tools that aren't on the connector list.

Where should a small business start?

Pick one job that repeats every week and already lives in a connected tool, like chasing unpaid invoices in QuickBooks. Leave approval on, and read every staged draft before you send it. You'll learn quickly whether the workflow understands your business, and you'll spot what to write into your rules before you hand over a second job.

That's how I built my own team too: one agent with one job and clear limits first, then the next one.
